Marginal Maximum Likelihood – irtlib.mml#

Marginal maximum likelihood calibration of the 1PL to 4PL with the EM algorithm, with optional priors.

Calibration has to estimate item parameters without the examinees’ abilities. Marginal maximum likelihood integrates the abilities out over a population distribution and maximizes the likelihood of the item parameters alone; Bock and Aitkin’s EM algorithm makes it feasible for any number of items, with one small problem per item in each cycle [Bock and Aitkin, 1981] (pp. 444, 447).

Ability is normal with mean 0 and variance 1 (ADR-0008), approximated on nodes \(X_q\) with masses \(A_q\) that sum to 1: the normal density at equally spaced nodes, normalized (Bock and Aitkin [1981], p. 449). Examinee \(j\)’s marginal probability is \(\tilde P_j = \sum_q L_j(X_q) A_q\), where \(L_j\) is the product of \(P_i^{u_{ij}} (1 - P_i)^{1 - u_{ij}}\) over the items \(i\) examinee \(j\) answered, and the marginal log-likelihood is \(\sum_j \log \tilde P_j\) (Bock and Aitkin [1981], pp. 445-446, Equations 6 and 8).

Each cycle has two steps (Baker [1987], p. 132, Equations 42-46; Bock and Aitkin [1981], p. 447):

  • the E-step computes, for each item and node, the expected number of examinees answering the item there, \(\bar N_{iq} = \sum_j L_j(X_q) A_q / \tilde P_j\) over the examinees who answered it, and the expected number answering it correctly, \(\bar r_{iq}\), the same sum weighted by \(u_{ij}\);

  • the M-step maximizes \(\sum_q [\bar r_{iq} \log P_i(X_q) + (\bar N_{iq} - \bar r_{iq}) \log Q_i(X_q)]\) in the item parameters, the grouped log-likelihood of irtlib.irt.

The prior masses enter only through the expected counts. With missing responses the sums run over the examinees who answered each item, so the expected sample size \(\bar N_{iq}\) is per item; the sources define it over complete response patterns, shared by every item.

The M-step solves in mirt’s parameters, the slope, the intercept and the logit of \(c\), by Fisher scoring with step halving (ADR-0007), on all free parameters at once: the system is one block per item unless a parameter is shared. The 1PL shares one slope, estimated, with abilities \(N(0, 1)\) (Thissen [1982], pp. 175-176, Equation 1); fixing the slope at 1 for every item gives the model with slopes 1 and abilities \(N(0, 1)\) (ADR-0008). No cycle decreases the marginal likelihood [Dempster et al., 1977] (p. 7, Theorem 1), and convergence is linear, slow near the solution (Dempster et al. [1977], p. 10; Bock and Aitkin [1981], p. 447). A calibration stops when no estimated parameter changes by more than its tolerance in a cycle (ADR-0007).

With priors on the item parameters, the estimates are Bayes modal: they maximize the log-likelihood plus the log-prior, and the EM algorithm carries over with the derivatives of the log-prior added to the M-step (Mislevy [1986], p. 182, Equation 14). The priors are mirt’s, on mirt’s parameters (ADR-0008): lognormal on the slope, whose log-density keeps the \(-\log a\) term, normal on the intercept, and normal on the logit of \(c\), which keeps \(c\) inside \((0, 1)\) (Mislevy [1986], p. 183). In the M-step’s scoring information, a log-prior’s negative second derivative counts only where it is positive, so the system stays positive definite. The 3PL’s likelihood is “nearly flat along one or more dimensions” (Mislevy [1986], p. 189), which a prior on the logit of \(c\) remedies. The 4PL adds an upper asymptote \(d < 1\), for examinees of high ability who still miss an item (Barton and Lord [1981], p. 2), estimated on its logit with a normal prior there; recovering it takes thousands of examinees (Waller and Feuerstahler [2017], p. 19; Culpepper [2016], Section 5).

An EM cycle is a fixed-point iteration \(x_{n+1} = F(x_n)\) of the vector \(x\) of free parameters; the largest eigenvalue of \(F\)’s Jacobian at the solution, the fraction of missing information, governs how slowly it converges (Du and Varadhan [2020], p. 3). By default a calibration accelerates it with SQUAREM (Du and Varadhan [2020], p. 4, Algorithm 1). From \(x_0\), two cycles give \(x_1 = F(x_0)\) and \(x_2 = F(x_1)\); with \(r = x_1 - x_0\), \(v = x_2 - x_1 - r\) and the steplength \(\alpha = -\lVert r \rVert / \lVert v \rVert\), the authors’ default (p. 4), the extrapolation is \(x_0 - 2 \alpha r + \alpha^2 v\). Where its log-posterior is above that of \(x_2\), Algorithm 1’s test with \(\eta = 0\), one cycle from it, the stabilization step, gives the next \(x_0\); elsewhere \(x_2\) does. No cycle then lowers the log-posterior, as no cycle of plain EM does (Dempster et al. [1977], p. 6). The norms are Euclidean over the free parameters, a shared slope counted once, and an extrapolation with a parameter that is not finite, or a guessing parameter at or above its upper asymptote, is not kept. The extrapolation is not a cycle: the stopping rule applies to every cycle, an EM step with or without acceleration. mirt 1.48 accelerates by Ramsay’s rule by default and offers SQUAREM (manual pp. 128-129); the maximum is the same (ADR-0007).

The standard errors are the square roots of the inverse observed information at the estimates, by Oakes’s identity [Oakes, 1999] (p. 480): the score of the marginal log-likelihood is the M-step’s gradient at the E-step’s own parameters (Equation 5), and its derivative as both move together is the observed-data Hessian, the sum of the complete-data Hessian and the missing information (Equation 6). That score, log-prior included, is differenced centrally with step \(10^{-5}\), the E-step recomputed at each shifted point, so the posterior precision takes in the log-prior’s second derivative (Mislevy [1986], p. 182, Equations 15-17); the result is symmetrized, which Chalmers [2018] (p. 17) finds improves it (ADR-0007). Chalmers (pp. 5-7), as mirt, differences only the missing information and takes the complete-data Hessian analytically; differencing the whole score keeps one path for every model and prior. The standard errors of \(a\), \(b = -\zeta / \lambda\), \(c\) and \(d\) follow by the delta method.

Starting values are the slope 0.851, the intercept \(\Phi^{-1}(p_i) \cdot 1.702 / \sqrt{0.75}\), or 2 where \(p_i\) is 0 or 1, the guessing parameter 0.15 and the upper asymptote 0.85, where \(p_i\) is item \(i\)’s proportion correct among the examinees who answered it: mirt’s starting values, computed over answered responses only (ADR-0007); Bock and Aitkin [1981] (p. 458) name the inverse normal of the proportion correct as a starting intercept.

class irtlib.mml.Acceleration(*values)[source]#

How MMLCalibrator accelerates its EM cycles.

"squarem" extrapolates from every two cycles and keeps an extrapolation only where its log-posterior is above the second cycle’s, so no cycle lowers the log-posterior; "none" runs plain EM. Both reach the same maximum by the same stopping rule, "squarem" in fewer cycles. A member equals its value, so either can be passed where an acceleration is expected.

Examples

>>> Acceleration("squarem") is Acceleration.SQUAREM
True
class irtlib.mml.MMLCalibrator(model: IrtModel | str = IrtModel.PL2, *, fixed_slope: float | None = None, priors: Priors | None = None, tolerance: float = 1e-06, max_cycles: int = 500, acceleration: Acceleration | str = Acceleration.SQUAREM, grid: QuadratureGrid | None = None)[source]#

Calibrate the 1PL to 4PL by marginal maximum likelihood with the EM algorithm, with optional priors.

Each call to calibrate() estimates the items of one response matrix, which may have missing responses. Abilities are normal with mean 0 and variance 1. With priors on the item parameters, the estimates are Bayes modal: they maximize the log-likelihood plus the log-prior.

Parameters:
modelirtlib.IrtModel or str, optional

"2PL", the default, estimates a slope per item; "1PL" one slope shared by every item; "3PL" also a guessing parameter per item, best with a guessing prior; "4PL" also an upper asymptote per item, best with priors on both asymptotes and thousands of examinees.

fixed_slopefloat, optional

A slope every item keeps instead of estimating it, with any model.

priorsPriors, optional

Priors on the slope, the intercept and the logits of the guessing parameter and upper asymptote of every item.

tolerancefloat, optional

The stopping rule: calibration stops when no estimated parameter changes by more than this in a cycle, an E-step and an M-step. Default is 1e-6.

max_cyclesint, optional

The most cycles a calibration runs before it stops unconverged. Default is 500.

accelerationAcceleration or str, optional

"squarem", the default, extrapolates between cycles to reach the same estimates in fewer of them; the extrapolations are not cycles. "none" runs plain EM.

gridirtlib.quadrature.QuadratureGrid, optional

The nodes the abilities are integrated over. Default is 61 equally spaced nodes on [-6, 6].

Raises:
ValueError

If fixed_slope is not finite and positive; a slope prior comes with fixed_slope, a guessing prior with a model other than the 3PL and 4PL, or an upper prior with a model other than the 4PL; tolerance is not positive; max_cycles is less than 1; or acceleration is not an Acceleration.

Examples

>>> import numpy
>>> responses = numpy.array([[1, 0, 1], [0, 0, 1], [1, 1, 0], [0, 1, 1], [1, 0, 0]], dtype=float)
>>> calibration = MMLCalibrator("1PL").calibrate(responses)
>>> calibration.converged, calibration.n_parameters
(True, 4)
calibrate(responses: NDArray[floating[Any]], start: ItemBank | None = None) → Calibration[source]#

Estimate the items of a response matrix.

Parameters:
responsesnumpy.ndarray

The response matrix: one row per examinee, one column per item, holding 1, 0 or NaN for a missing response.

startirtlib.ItemBank, optional

Items to start from, one per column; the 1PL starts from their mean slope. By default, the slope 0.851 and an intercept from each item’s proportion correct.

Returns:
Calibration

The estimates, their standard errors and the log-likelihood at them, with the cycles run, which do not count the extrapolations of an acceleration, and the largest change of a parameter in the last one. If the stopping rule is not met within max_cycles, the result says so and a ConvergenceWarning is issued; if the information matrix cannot be inverted, the result has no standard errors and a RuntimeWarning is issued; if an item’s estimates are not valid in an item bank, such as a miskeyed item’s slope that is not positive, the result’s bank leaves the item out and a RuntimeWarning names it.

Raises:
TypeError

If the responses are neither Booleans, integers nor floats.

ValueError

If the responses fail irtlib.calibration.validate_responses(); an item was answered correctly by every examinee who answered it or by none, which leaves it no finite estimate; the expected information of the parameters is singular; or start has a different number of items, or leaves an item’s guessing parameter at or above its upper asymptote.

Priors on the parameters of every item, each given as a mean and a standard deviation (ADR-0008).

A prior applies to each item’s parameter, and once to a parameter the items share, such as the 1PL’s slope.

The mean and standard deviation of the log of every slope: a lognormal prior on the slope.

intercepttuple[float, float] or None

The mean and standard deviation of a normal prior on every intercept, -a * b. It keeps the estimates of items answered correctly by everyone or by no one finite.

guessingtuple[float, float] or None

The mean and standard deviation of a normal prior on the logit of every guessing parameter; for the 3PL and 4PL.
