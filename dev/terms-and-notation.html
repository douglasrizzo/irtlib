Terms and Notation#

The irtlib documentation uses one term for each concept. This page lists each term with the synonyms you may know from the literature and the name to look for in the API, then the symbols that the formulas use.

Examinees and Items#

examinee

The person who answers a test’s items. Also called respondent, subject or person. In the API an examinee is a row of the response matrix.

ability \(\theta\)

The characteristic a test measures, on a continuous scale. Also called proficiency, latent trait or person parameter. In the API: theta.

ability estimate \(\hat{\theta}\)

The estimate of an examinee’s ability from its responses. In the API: the theta of an AbilityEstimates.

item

One question of a test, scored correct or incorrect. In the API an item is a column of the response matrix and a row \([a, b, c, d]\) of an item matrix, items; in an item bank it also has a name, unique in its bank (name).

item bank

The calibrated items of a test, with their names. Also called item pool. In the API: irtlib.ItemBank, which holds \(M\) items, n_items, and their names.

logistic models

The item response models irtlib implements: the one-, two-, three- and four-parameter logistic models (1PL to 4PL). The 1PL with every discrimination equal to 1 is the Rasch model. In the API: irtlib.IrtModel, and an item bank’s model.

discrimination \(a_i\)

How sharply item \(i\) separates examinees of lower and higher ability: the slope of its characteristic curve. Also called the slope parameter. ItemBank.discrimination.

difficulty \(b_i\)

The ability at which item \(i\)’s characteristic curve is steepest, on the same scale as \(\theta\). Also called the location or threshold. ItemBank.difficulty.

guessing parameter \(c_i\)

The lower asymptote of item \(i\)’s characteristic curve: the probability that an examinee of very low ability answers correctly. Also called the pseudo-guessing parameter. ItemBank.pseudo_guessing; 0 under the 1PL and 2PL.

upper asymptote \(d_i\)

The upper asymptote of item \(i\)’s characteristic curve: the probability that an examinee of very high ability answers correctly. \(1 - d_i\) is called the slipping or carelessness parameter. ItemBank.upper_asymptote; 1 under every model but the 4PL.

item characteristic curve \(P_i(\theta)\)

The probability of a correct response to item \(i\) at ability \(\theta\). Also called the item response function (IRF). irtlib.irt.icc().

item information \(I_i(\theta)\)

The Fisher information of a response to item \(i\) about the ability at \(\theta\). irtlib.irt.information().

test information \(I(\theta)\)

The sum of the information of a set of items. irtlib.irt.total_information().

standard error \(SE(\hat{\theta})\)

The uncertainty of an ability estimate, on the scale of \(\theta\). Also called the standard error of estimation (SEE) or standard error of measurement (SEM). Each estimator computes it by its own formula; for maximum likelihood it is \(1/\sqrt{I(\hat{\theta})}\). In the API: the standard_errors of an AbilityEstimates, and irtlib.irt.see().

Responses#

response \(u_{ij}\)

Examinee \(j\)’s response to item \(i\): 1 if correct, 0 if not.

response matrix

The responses of a sample of examinees, with one row per examinee and one column per item, and NaN where an examinee did not answer an item. Also called the data matrix. In the API: responses. The responses are complete when no entry is missing.

number correct

The number of items an examinee answered correctly. Also called the raw score or number-correct score.

rest score

An examinee’s number correct on the items other than the one in question.

proportion correct

The share of the examinees who answered an item that answered it correctly. Also called the item’s p-value. In the API: proportion_correct().

item-rest correlation

The correlation of an item’s responses with the examinees’ rest scores, a point-biserial correlation. In the API: item_rest_correlation().

Calibration#

calibration

Estimating the items’ parameters from a response matrix. Also called item parameter estimation. These pages never say “estimation” alone where calibration and ability estimation could be confused.

calibrator

An object that calibrates items by one method: MMLCalibrator, CMLCalibrator or JMLCalibrator. Its calibrate method returns a Calibration.

marginal, conditional and joint likelihood

The likelihoods the three calibration methods maximize: with the abilities integrated out over a population distribution (marginal), given each examinee’s number correct (conditional), or given every examinee’s ability, estimated with the items (joint). The methods are marginal maximum likelihood (MML), conditional maximum likelihood (CML) and joint maximum likelihood (JML). In the API: Likelihood.

quadrature grid

The abilities that calibration and ability estimation integrate over, the nodes \(X_q\), \(q = 1, \dots, Q\), each with a weight. Also called quadrature points. In the API: QuadratureGrid.

ability prior

A density of ability: the population distribution that marginal maximum likelihood integrates over, or the prior of a Bayesian ability estimate. Its mass \(A_q\) at node \(X_q\) is proportional to the node’s weight times the density there, and the masses sum to 1. In the API: normal_log_prior(), prior_masses().

item priors

Priors on the item parameters, under which the calibrated parameters are Bayes modal estimates: they maximize the likelihood times the priors. In the API: Priors.

cycle

One round of a calibration’s algorithm, such as an EM cycle. In the API: the cycles of a Calibration; converged says whether its stopping rule was met.

Ability Estimation#

ability estimation

Estimating each examinee’s ability from its responses to items of known parameters.

estimator

A method of ability estimation: maximum likelihood (ML), maximum a posteriori (MAP, also called Bayes modal), expected a posteriori (EAP) or Warm’s weighted likelihood (WLE). In the API: the functions of irtlib.ability.

posterior

An examinee’s distribution of ability given its responses: proportional to the prior times the likelihood. In the API: posterior(), over the nodes of a grid.

Checking a Calibration#

item fit

How well the responses a calibration predicts for an item match those observed. In the API: s_x2() and mean_squares().

person fit

How well an examinee’s responses match their expectations at the examinee’s ability. In the API: person_mean_squares().

model comparison

Whether a model with more parameters fits enough better to justify them. In the API: the functions of irtlib.comparison.
