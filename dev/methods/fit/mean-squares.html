Mean Squares#

API:

irtlib.fit.mean_squares(); irtlib.fit.MeanSquares

Sources:

Wright and Masters [1982]

Motivation#

The weighted and unweighted mean squares compare each response with its expectation at the examinee’s estimated ability [Wright and Masters, 1982, pp. 97-99], summed item by item: the infit and the outfit of the Rasch model. Wright and Masters define them for the models of their book, all built from the Rasch model (p. 10; p. 100, Table 5.4a), whose model for dichotomous responses (p. 38, Equation 3.1.1) is the 1PL, every item with the same slope \(a\), with person parameters \(a\theta_j\) and item parameters \(ab_i\). For the other models, S-X² tests item fit.

Definition#

For examinee \(j\) and item \(i\), with \(P_{ij}\) the probability of a correct response at the examinee’s ability and \(Q_{ij} = 1 - P_{ij}\), the score residual \(y_{ij} = u_{ij} - P_{ij}\) [Wright and Masters, 1982, p. 98, Equation 5.4.2] has variance \(W_{ij}\) and kurtosis \(C_{ij}\) (p. 98, Equations 5.4.3 and 5.4.4), which for the two categories of a dichotomous item are

\[W_{ij} = P_{ij} Q_{ij}, \quad C_{ij} = P_{ij} Q_{ij} (P_{ij}^3 + Q_{ij}^3),\]

and the standardized residual is \(z_{ij} = y_{ij} / W_{ij}^{1/2}\) (p. 99, Equation 5.4.5). Over the \(N\) examinees, the unweighted and the weighted mean squares, which Wright and Masters write \(u_i\) and \(v_i\), are

\[U_i = \frac{1}{N} \sum_j z_{ij}^2, \quad V_i = \frac{\sum_j W_{ij} z_{ij}^2}{\sum_j W_{ij}} = \frac{\sum_j y_{ij}^2}{\sum_j W_{ij}}\]

(p. 99, Equations 5.4.6 and 5.4.7). irtlib calls \(U_i\) the outfit and \(V_i\) the infit. \(U_i\) is sensitive to unexpected responses of examinees for whom the item is far too easy or far too hard, and the weights \(W_{ij}\), smallest for those examinees, lessen their influence on \(V_i\) (pp. 99, 101). When the data fit the model both have expectation 1, \(U_i\) with variance \(\sum_j (C_{ij} / W_{ij}^2) / N^2 - 1 / N\) (p. 100, Table 5.4a) and \(V_i\) with variance

\[q_i^2 = \frac{\sum_j (C_{ij} - W_{ij}^2)}{\left(\sum_j W_{ij}\right)^2}\]

(p. 101, Equation 5.4.8). The weighted mean square is standardized to

\[t_i = (V_i^{1/3} - 1)(3 / q_i) + q_i / 3,\]

which has a mean near 0 and a standard deviation near 1 when the data fit (p. 101; p. 100, Table 5.4a). Wright and Masters estimate the person and item parameters from the same data as the residuals, which lowers the expected mean square by the factor \((M - 1)(N - 1) / (MN)\) for \(M\) items (p. 101).

Implementation#

The abilities are the EAP estimates of irtlib.ability.expected_a_posteriori() on the calibration’s grid, abilities normal with mean 0 and variance 1 (ADR-0006), so the mean squares take a calibration of the 1PL by Marginal Maximum Likelihood.

Three choices no source on the shelf covers:

  • the unweighted mean square is standardized in the same way as the weighted one, with the standard deviation from Table 5.4a in place of \(q_i\);

  • a variance \(W_{ij}\) whose square is below \(10^{-5}\) is raised to \(10^{-5/2}\), leaving \(C_{ij}\) and \(y_{ij}\) as they are, so that a mean square’s variance can fall below 0, and its \(t\) is then NaN;

  • the standard deviation of either mean square is capped at 1.4142 before standardizing.

The sources wanted list all three.

Complete responses. Wright and Masters’ sums run over every examinee, with no rule for a missing response, so irtlib’s mean squares need complete responses (ADR-0006).

Comparison with mirt

  • mirt 1.48 calls \(U_i\) and \(V_i\) outfit and infit too (R/itemfit.R:584, 588), and takes EAP estimates by default (R/itemfit.R:268, 535-538), on the grid of its fscores.

  • irtlib’s three choices above are mirt’s: the standardized outfit (R/itemfit.R:585, 587), the floor on \(W_{ij}\) (R/personfit.R:189-200) and the cap on the standard deviation (R/itemfit.R:586, 591).

  • mirt computes the mean squares of any model from its probabilities (R/personfit.R:181-194), and with missing responses sums over the examinees who answered each item, with \(N\) their number (R/itemfit.R:577-584).

  • At mirt’s estimates and abilities the parity tests find the mean squares and their standardized values within a relative \(10^{-10}\); on irtlib’s own calibration, the mean squares within a relative \(10^{-4}\) and the standardized values within \(10^{-3}\) (ADR-0003).

Parameters#

mean_squares() takes the calibration and the responses it was calibrated on, and has no settings.

Guarantees#

  1. Each item gets its outfit and infit, and their standardized values; a standardized value is NaN where its mean square’s variance is not positive.

  2. A missing response, a calibration whose likelihood is not marginal, or a calibration of a model other than the 1PL raises an error.

References#

API Reference#

irtlib.fit.mean_squares(calibration: Calibration, responses: NDArray[floating[Any]]) → MeanSquares[source]#

Return the outfit and infit mean squares of every item of a 1PL calibration, from its responses.

Each response is compared with its probability at the examinee’s EAP ability estimate on the calibration’s grid, with abilities normal with mean 0 and variance 1.

The Mean Squares page gives the method, its sources and the origin of each default.

Parameters:
calibrationCalibration

A calibration of the 1PL, whose items all have the same slope, c = 0 and d = 1, of the marginal likelihood, on whose grid the abilities are estimated.

responsesnumpy.ndarray

The complete response matrix the calibration estimated its items from: one row per examinee, one column per item, 1 or 0.

Returns:
MeanSquares

Each item’s outfit and infit, and their standardized values.

Raises:
TypeError

If the responses are neither Booleans, integers nor floats.

ValueError

If the calibration’s items are not those of a 1PL, or its likelihood is not marginal, so that it has no grid; or the responses fail irtlib.calibration.validate_responses(), have a missing response, or do not have one column per item of the calibration.

Examples

>>> import numpy
>>> from irtlib.drawing import draw_responses
>>> from irtlib.mml import MMLCalibrator
>>> items = numpy.column_stack([numpy.ones(4), [-1.0, -0.3, 0.3, 1.0], numpy.zeros(4), numpy.ones(4)])
>>> responses = draw_responses(numpy.random.default_rng(1).normal(size=500), items, rng=2)
>>> fit = mean_squares(MMLCalibrator("1PL").calibrate(responses), responses)
>>> fit.outfit.round(3), fit.infit.round(3)
(array([0.751, 0.772, 0.741, 0.723]), array([0.821, 0.807, 0.777, 0.791]))
class irtlib.fit.MeanSquares(outfit: NDArray[floating[Any]], outfit_t: NDArray[floating[Any]], infit: NDArray[floating[Any]], infit_t: NDArray[floating[Any]])[source]#

The outfit and infit mean squares of each item, each with its standardized value.

Both mean squares have expectation 1 when the responses fit the model, and less when the abilities are estimated from the same responses, as they are here.

Parameters:
outfitnumpy.ndarray

The unweighted mean square of each item, Wright and Masters’ \(u_i\): the mean, over examinees, of the squared standardized residuals.

outfit_tnumpy.ndarray

The outfit standardized by the same cube-root transformation as infit_t, with the outfit’s own standard deviation under the model; NaN where its variance under the model is not positive.

infitnumpy.ndarray

The weighted mean square of each item, Wright and Masters’ \(v_i\): the squared residuals summed over examinees, over the sum of their variances.

infit_tnumpy.ndarray

The infit standardized by a cube-root transformation, with its standard deviation under the model; NaN where its variance under the model is not positive.
