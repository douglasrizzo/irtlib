S-X²#

API:

irtlib.fit.s_x2(); irtlib.fit.ItemFit

Sources:

Orlando and Thissen [2000]

Motivation#

Orlando and Thissen’s S-X² tests, item by item, whether a calibrated model predicts the responses observed. It groups examinees by their number-correct score, which is observed, rather than by an ability estimate, whose groups depend on the model [Orlando and Thissen, 2000, p. 52], and it applies to every model from the 1PL to the 4PL. For the Rasch model, Mean Squares compare each response with its expectation instead.

Definition#

For item \(i\) of \(M\) and score \(k = 1, \dots, M - 1\), with \(N_k\) examinees at score \(k\) and \(O_{ik}\) the observed proportion of them answering item \(i\) correctly,

\[S\text{-}X^2_i = \sum_{k=1}^{M-1} N_k \frac{(O_{ik} - E_{ik})^2}{E_{ik}(1 - E_{ik})}\]

[Orlando and Thissen, 2000, p. 54, Equation 13]. The expected proportion is

\[E_{ik} = \frac{\sum_q P_i(X_q)\, S^{*i}_{k-1}(X_q)\, A_q}{\sum_q S_k(X_q)\, A_q}\]

(p. 54, Equation 12), where \(S_k(X_q)\) is the likelihood of a score of \(k\) at ability \(X_q\) and \(S^{*i}_k\) the same without item \(i\), both from Lord and Wingersky’s recursion over items (p. 53, Equations 6-11): adding an item with probability \(T\) turns \(S^*_k\) into \(T S^*_{k-1} + (1 - T) S^*_k\). The statistic sums \((O - E)^2 / E\) over the cells of the table of scores by responses, and its degrees of freedom are the rows of the table less the number of parameters estimated for the item (p. 56).

Implementation#

The grid. The nodes and their normal masses \(A_q\) are the calibration’s grid, so S-X² takes a calibration by Marginal Maximum Likelihood (ADR-0006).

Collapsing the table. Orlando and Thissen collapse the rows from both ends toward the middle until every expected count is at least 1 [Orlando and Thissen, 2000, p. 56]. irtlib collapses the two cells of each score row, incorrect and correct: rows no examinee reached are dropped, a row with one examinee is merged into the next, the last into the one before, and while an expected count is below min_expected, the first such row is merged into its neighbour, the first row into the second, the last into the one before and any other into the neighbour with fewer examinees. The degrees of freedom are floored at 0. An item whose collapsed table leaves no degrees of freedom, including one collapsed to a single row, has none and no statistic.

Complete responses. S-X² “cannot handle incomplete data directly” [Zhang and Wang, 2022, p. 705], so it needs complete responses (ADR-0006).

Comparison with mirt

  • The collapsing is mirt 1.48’s (R/utils.R:2410-2546); the parity tests check the collapsed expected counts against mirt’s within a relative \(10^{-13}\).

  • At mirt’s estimates, the statistics and \(p\)-values agree with mirt’s within a relative \(10^{-10}\); on irtlib’s own calibration, within a relative \(10^{-4}\) (ADR-0003).

  • mirt skips an item whose table collapses to a single row; irtlib reports it with no degrees of freedom and no statistic.

Guarantees#

  1. Every item of the calibration gets a statistic, the items its bank leaves out included, unless its collapsed table leaves no degrees of freedom.

  2. A missing response, or a calibration whose likelihood is not marginal, raises an error.

API Reference#

irtlib.fit.s_x2(calibration: Calibration, responses: NDArray[floating[Any]], *, min_expected: float = 1.0) → ItemFit[source]#

Return the S-X² item-fit statistic of every item of a calibration, from the responses it was calibrated on.

Every item is evaluated at its estimates, including the items the calibration’s bank leaves out.

The S-X² page gives the method, its sources and the origin of each default.

Parameters:
calibrationCalibration

The calibration whose items are evaluated, of the marginal likelihood, whose grid the expected counts are integrated over.

responsesnumpy.ndarray

The complete response matrix the calibration estimated its items from: one row per examinee, one column per item, 1 or 0.

min_expectedfloat, optional

The smallest expected count a cell may keep after collapsing, at least 0. Default is 1. The expected counts are integrated over the calibration’s grid.

Returns:
ItemFit

Each item’s statistic, degrees of freedom and p-value.

Raises:
TypeError

If the responses are neither Booleans, integers nor floats.

ValueError

If the responses fail irtlib.calibration.validate_responses(), have a missing response, or do not have one column per item of the calibration; min_expected is negative; or the calibration’s likelihood is not marginal, so that it has no grid.

class irtlib.fit.ItemFit(statistic: NDArray[floating[Any]], df: NDArray[integer[Any]], p_value: NDArray[floating[Any]])[source]#
