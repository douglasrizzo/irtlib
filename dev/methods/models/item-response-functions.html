Item Response Functions#

API:

irtlib.irt

Sources:

Magis [2013]; Magis and Raîche [2012]; Barton and Lord [1981]; Baker [1987]; Veerkamp and Berger [1994]; Barrada et al. [2006]

Motivation#

Every method in irtlib rests on the same few quantities of the logistic models: the probability of a correct response, its derivatives, the information an item gives about the ability, and the likelihood of a set of responses. irtlib.irt computes them once, for every model from the 1PL to the 4PL, and the calibrators, the ability estimators and item fit call it, as catsim does. A package that builds on irtlib can call it too.

Definition#

Under the four-parameter logistic model, an examinee of ability \(\theta\) answers item \(i\) correctly with probability

\[P_i(\theta) = c_i + (d_i - c_i) F_i(\theta), \quad F_i(\theta) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-a_i(\theta - b_i)}}\]

[Magis, 2013, p. 306, Equation 1], and incorrectly with probability \(Q_i = 1 - P_i\). The 3PL fixes \(d_i = 1\), and the 2PL also \(c_i = 0\) [Magis and Raîche, 2012, p. 3]. The derivatives of \(P_i\) with respect to the ability are

\[P_i' = a_i (d_i - c_i) F_i (1 - F_i), \quad P_i'' = a_i P_i' (1 - 2 F_i),\]

the derivatives of Barton and Lord [1981, p. A2] written with \(F_i\) and their constant \(D\) set to 1; the first is also Magis [2013, p. 306, Equations 4 and 5].

Information. The information item \(i\) gives about the ability is \(I_i = P_i'^2 / (P_i Q_i)\) [Magis and Raîche, 2012, p. 4, Equation 4], for the 4PL

\[I_i(\theta) = \frac{a_i^2 (P_i - c_i)^2 (d_i - P_i)^2}{(d_i - c_i)^2 P_i Q_i}\]

[Magis, 2013, p. 307, Equation 6], and the test information \(I(\theta)\) is the sum of the information of its items [Magis and Raîche, 2012, p. 5, Equation 8].

Standard error and confidence interval. The maximum likelihood estimate of the ability is asymptotically normal, with variance the inverse of the test information [Warm, 1989, p. 445] and standard error \(1 / \sqrt{I(\theta)}\) [Magis and Raîche, 2012, p. 4, Equation 3]. confidence_interval() is the interval this normal approximation gives, \(\hat\theta \pm z \cdot SE\), with \(z\) the standard normal’s quantile at \((1 + \text{confidence}) / 2\).

Reliability. The reliability of the test at \(\theta\) is \(I(\theta) / (I(\theta) + 1 / \sigma^2)\), with the abilities’ variance \(\sigma^2 = 1\), as they are \(N(0, 1)\) (ADR-0008). For abilities \(N(0, 1)\), Bock and Mislevy [1982, p. 437, Equation 7] define a related reliability of the EAP estimate, one minus its posterior variance.

The ability of maximum information. An item is most informative at

\[\theta^*_i = b_i + \frac{1}{a_i} \log \frac{x^*_i - c_i}{d_i - x^*_i}, \quad x^*_i = 2 \sqrt{\frac{-u_i}{3}} \cos\left\{\frac{1}{3} \arccos\left(-\frac{v_i}{2} \sqrt{\frac{27}{-u_i^3}}\right) + \frac{4 \pi}{3}\right\} + \frac{1}{2},\]

with \(u_i = -3/4 + (c_i + d_i - 2 c_i d_i)/2\) and \(v_i = (c_i + d_i - 1)/4\), where \(x^*_i\) is the probability of a correct response at \(\theta^*_i\) [Magis, 2013, pp. 308-309, Equations 13, 18 and 19]. With \(d_i = 1\) it is \(\theta^*_i = b_i + \log[(1 + \sqrt{1 + 8 c_i}) / 2] / a_i\) (p. 310, Equation 23), and with \(c_i + d_i = 1\) it is \(b_i\) (pp. 309-310). The 3PL’s \(\theta^*_i\) is also Equation 2 of Barrada et al. [2006, p. 157], with their constant \(D = 1.7\).

The integral of the information. Since \(P_i' = a_i (P_i - c_i)(d_i - P_i) / (d_i - c_i)\) [Magis, 2013, p. 306, Equation 5], changing the variable of integration from \(\theta\) to \(P_i\) in the information and splitting the result into partial fractions integrates an item’s information in closed form:

\[\int_{\theta_L}^{\theta_U} I_i(\theta)\, d\theta = \frac{a_i}{d_i - c_i} \Big[ P_i - c_i d_i \log P_i + (1 - c_i)(1 - d_i) \log(1 - P_i) \Big]_{P_i(\theta_L)}^{P_i(\theta_U)},\]

which extends the 3PL closed form of Veerkamp and Berger [1994, p. 22, Equation 25], to which it reduces when \(d_i = 1\). Under the 2PL the integral over the whole scale is \(a_i\) (p. 22, Equation 28).

The log-likelihood. The log-likelihood of examinee \(j\)’s responses \(u_{ij}\) is

\[\log L_j(\theta) = \sum_i [u_{ij} \log P_i(\theta) + (1 - u_{ij}) \log Q_i(\theta)]\]

over the items \(i\) the examinee answered [Magis and Raîche, 2012, p. 4, Equation 2]; [Barton and Lord, 1981, p. A1].

A reporting scale. theta_to_scale() takes abilities to scores on a reporting scale along the line through \((\theta_{min}, \text{scale}_{min})\) and \((\theta_{max}, \text{scale}_{max})\), and scale_to_theta() takes them back.

Implementation#

Items and shapes. The functions take one item as a row [a, b, c, d] of shape (4,), or several as a matrix of such rows, shape (n, 4), with \(a > 0\) and \(0 \leq c < d \leq 1\), and a response matrix with one row per examinee and one column per item, holding 1, 0 or NaN where the examinee did not answer the item. The models have no scaling constant (ADR-0001).

The derivatives calibration uses. Calibration solves for each item’s parameters, and reports their standard errors, from the derivatives of \(P_i\) with respect to those parameters, which every method that estimates items shares. It estimates them in four parameters (ADR-0007): the slope \(\lambda = a\) and the intercept \(\zeta = -ab\), so that \(a(\theta - b) = \lambda\theta + \zeta\), and the logits \(\gamma\) of \(c\) and \(\delta\) of \(d\). Every array of these derivatives has the four on its last axis, in that order. By the chain rule,

\[\frac{\partial P}{\partial \lambda} = (d - c)\,\theta\,F(1 - F), \quad \frac{\partial P}{\partial \zeta} = (d - c)\,F(1 - F), \quad \frac{\partial P}{\partial \gamma} = c(1 - c)(1 - F), \quad \frac{\partial P}{\partial \delta} = d(1 - d)\,F,\]

from the derivatives in \(a\), \(b\) and \(c\) of Barton and Lord [1981, p. A3] and the derivative of the logistic function. A parameter at its bound, \(c = 0\) or \(d = 1\), has derivative 0.

For an item answered by \(N_q\) examinees at ability \(X_q\), \(r_q\) of them correctly, the log-likelihood is \(\sum_q [r_q \log P(X_q) + (N_q - r_q)\log Q(X_q)]\), the grouped form of Baker [1987, p. 112, Equation 3]. Its derivative in a parameter \(\chi\) is \(\sum_q [r_q - N_q P(X_q)]\,(\partial P / \partial\chi) / (PQ)\), the form of Barton and Lord [1981, p. A2] summed within each group; with \(d = 1\) the derivative in \(\gamma\) is the equation for the logit of \(c\) of Mislevy [1986, p. 184, Equation 22]. The expected information of one response is \((\partial P / \partial\chi)(\partial P / \partial\phi) / (PQ)\), Barton and Lord’s second derivative (p. A2) without its terms in \(u - P\), whose expected value is 0. For the 2PL in these parameters the observed information equals the expected one [Baker, 1987, p. 116].

Precision far from the difficulty. Nothing is computed by subtracting from 1. \(F\) and \(1 - F\) both come from the logit; \(P = c + (d - c)F\) and \(Q = (1 - d) + (d - c)(1 - F)\); \(\log P\) and \(\log Q\) combine \(\log c\) or \(\log(1 - d)\) with \(\log(d - c)\) and \(\log F\) or \(\log(1 - F)\). The ratios \((d - c)F / P\), \((d - c)(1 - F) / Q\), \(c / P\) and \((1 - d) / Q\) each lie between 0 and 1. The first is 0 / 0 only where \(c = 0\) and \(F\) has underflowed to 0, the second only where \(d = 1\) and \(1 - F\) has, and both take their limit there, 1. With \(P - c = (d - c)F\) and \(d - P = (d - c)(1 - F)\) [Magis, 2013, p. 306, Equation 3], the item information is \(a^2 F (1 - F)\) times the first two ratios.

Departures from the sources. Magis assumes \(0 < c_i < 0.5\) and \(0.5 < d_i < 1\) [Magis, 2013, p. 306]; max_info_theta() takes his Equation 19 for every item with \(d_i < 1\), and his Equation 23 for every item with \(d_i = 1\). The closed form of the integral of the information with \(d_i < 1\) has no published source; the sources wanted list both.

Comparison with mirt and catR

  • The reliability is mirt 1.48’s (R/SingleGroup-methods.R:1474), the curve that plot(type = "rxx") draws; the parity tests check it against mirt’s recorded values within a relative \(10^{-10}\).

  • The closed-form integral of the information agrees with mirt 1.48’s areainfo, which integrates numerically (R/areainfo.R:49-66), within the same tolerance.

  • The parameters calibration solves in are mirt’s (ADR-0007).

  • The probabilities and the information agree with catR 3.17’s on the recorded abilities and items within a relative \(10^{-10}\), after the recorded values are checked against Magis’s equations written out (ADR-0003).

Guarantees#

  1. Probabilities, information and log-likelihoods are finite at any finite ability, however far from an item’s difficulty.

  2. Items without information, or no items at all, give a test information of 0.

  3. A guessing parameter of 0 or an upper asymptote of 1 has derivative 0 in calibration’s parameters.

API Reference#

The item response functions of the 1PL to 4PL models, and the quantities derived from them.

The functions take one item as a row [a, b, c, d] of shape (4,), or several as a matrix of such rows, shape (n, 4), with a > 0 and 0 <= c < d <= 1, and a response matrix with one row per examinee and one column per item, holding 1, 0 or NaN where the examinee did not answer the item.

irtlib.irt.THETA_MAX_EXTENDED = 6.0#

The upper end of the extended ability range, which the default quadrature grid spans.

irtlib.irt.THETA_MAX_TYPICAL = 4.0#

The upper end of the typical ability range, the default theta_max of theta_to_scale().

irtlib.irt.THETA_MIN_EXTENDED = -6.0#

The lower end of the extended ability range, which the default quadrature grid spans.

irtlib.irt.THETA_MIN_TYPICAL = -4.0#

The lower end of the typical ability range, the default theta_min of theta_to_scale().
