Quadrature and Ability Priors#

API:

irtlib.quadrature

Sources:

Bock and Mislevy [1982]; Bock and Aitkin [1981]

Motivation#

Marginal maximum likelihood integrates over the abilities of a population, and the expected a posteriori estimate integrates over one examinee’s posterior. Neither integral has a closed form, so both become sums over the nodes of a grid of abilities. irtlib.quadrature holds that grid, the ability priors it is weighted by, and each examinee’s posterior over it, shared by calibration and ability estimation.

Definition#

Calibration and ability estimation both replace the integral over ability by a sum over the nodes \(X_q\), \(q = 1, \dots, Q\), of a grid. Each node carries a mass \(A_q\), the probability of a discrete prior distribution at that node, and the masses sum to 1 [Bock and Mislevy, 1982, p. 433, Equation 6]. A grid holds the nodes and a weight \(w_q\) for each, and a prior is a log-density \(\log \pi\). A node’s mass is its weight times the prior density there, normalized:

\[A_q = \frac{w_q\,\pi(X_q)}{\sum_{h=1}^{Q} w_h\,\pi(X_h)}.\]

Examinee \(j\)’s likelihood at a node, \(L_j(X_q)\), is the product over the items \(i\) it answered of \(P_i(X_q)^{u_{ij}} [1 - P_i(X_q)]^{1 - u_{ij}}\) [Bock and Mislevy, 1982, p. 433, Equation 3], and its posterior mass at the node is

\[\frac{L_j(X_q)\,A_q}{\sum_{h=1}^{Q} L_j(X_h)\,A_h}.\]

The mean of the posterior over the nodes is the EAP estimate of the examinee’s ability, and its variance serves as the estimate’s error variance: the square of the posterior standard deviation [Bock and Mislevy, 1982, p. 432]; [Bock and Mislevy, 1982, p. 433, Equations 4 and 5].

Implementation#

The grid. The equal weights of uniform() cancel, which leaves the prior density at the nodes, normalized: the masses Bock and Aitkin [1981, p. 449] take for a normal prior, and for any other. Those nodes are evenly spaced, as Bock and Mislevy’s are: Gauss-Hermite quadrature, they note, has less advantage for the likelihoods of adaptive testing [Bock and Mislevy, 1982, p. 433]. The default grid has 121 nodes on the extended ability range \([-6, 6]\), a spacing of 0.1: about the spacing of the 80 points between \(\pm 4\) standard deviations that Bock and Mislevy (p. 433) suggest to keep precision down to a posterior standard deviation of 0.2. Calibration uses its own grid, 61 nodes on the same range (ADR-0007).

The posterior. An examinee who answered no item has \(L_j = 1\) at every node, and its posterior is the masses. The log-likelihoods accumulate as sums over the items [Bock and Mislevy, 1982, p. 432], so posterior() takes each examinee’s log-likelihood at the nodes, whatever the response function that gave it. It adds the log of each node’s weight and prior density, and shifts each examinee’s row by its maximum before exponentiating, so that the likelihood of a long response pattern does not underflow. A GridPrior evaluates and checks a prior on a grid’s nodes once, for a caller that computes many posteriors with the same grid and prior.

Departure from the source. Bock and Mislevy apply Sheppard’s correction to the posterior variance [Bock and Mislevy, 1982, p. 433]; posterior_variance() does not.

Comparison with mirt and catR

  • The range \([-6, 6]\) is mirt 1.48’s default theta_lim [Chalmers, 2026, p. 129].

  • The EAP estimates that catR 3.17 records are checked to be the posterior mean and standard deviation written out, on the same nodes and weights, within a relative \(10^{-10}\), before irtlib is compared with them (ADR-0003).

Guarantees#

  1. The masses of a grid under a prior are non-negative and sum to 1; a node outside a uniform prior’s interval gets mass 0.

  2. An examinee who answered no item gets the prior’s masses as its posterior.

  3. The posterior of a long response pattern does not underflow to zero at every node.

class irtlib.quadrature.GridPrior(grid: QuadratureGrid, log_prior: LogPrior)[source]#

A prior over the nodes of a grid, evaluated and checked once, for posteriors computed many times on them.

It keeps copies of the grid’s nodes and of each node’s log weight plus log prior density, so changing the grid’s arrays afterwards does not change it. posterior() returns what posterior() returns for the same grid and prior, without evaluating the prior again.

Parameters:
gridQuadratureGrid

The nodes and their weights.

log_priorLogPrior

Vectorized log-density of the prior.

Raises:
ValueError

If the log-prior is NaN or +inf at a node, or -inf at every node.

Examples

>>> import numpy
>>> prior = GridPrior(QuadratureGrid.uniform(3, -1.0, 1.0), uniform_log_prior(-1.0, 1.0))
>>> prior.posterior(numpy.log(numpy.array([[0.2, 0.5, 0.8]]))).round(4)
array([[0.1333, 0.3333, 0.5333]])

class irtlib.quadrature.QuadratureGrid(nodes: NDArray[floating[Any]], weights: NDArray[floating[Any]])[source]#

The nodes abilities are integrated over, each with a weight.

A node’s mass is proportional to its weight times the prior density there, so the weights take part in the posterior itself; equal weights, as uniform() builds, cancel in the normalization. Two grids are equal only if they are the same object, and a grid can be a set member or a dictionary key.

Parameters:
nodesnumpy.ndarray

One-dimensional array of finite abilities.

weightsnumpy.ndarray

One-dimensional array of finite, positive weights, one per node.

Raises:
ValueError

If the arrays are not one-dimensional, differ in length, have fewer than two entries, or hold nodes that are not finite or weights that are not finite and positive.

irtlib.quadrature.posterior(log_likelihood: NDArray[floating[Any]], grid: QuadratureGrid, log_prior: LogPrior) → NDArray[floating[Any]][source]#

Return each examinee’s posterior over the nodes of a grid, from its log-likelihood at the nodes.

An examinee’s posterior mass at a node is proportional to the node’s weight times the prior density and the likelihood there. A row of zeros, the log-likelihood of an examinee who answered no item, gives the prior’s masses.

Parameters:
log_likelihoodnumpy.ndarray

Each examinee’s log-likelihood at each node, shape (examinees, nodes); -inf where the likelihood is 0.

gridQuadratureGrid

The nodes and their weights.

log_priorLogPrior

Vectorized log-density of the prior.

Returns:
numpy.ndarray

The posterior probabilities, shape (examinees, nodes), each row summing to 1.

Raises:
ValueError

If log_likelihood is not of shape (examinees, nodes), if the log-prior or the log-likelihood is NaN or +inf at a node, or if an examinee’s posterior has no mass at any node.

Examples

>>> import numpy
>>> grid = QuadratureGrid.uniform(3, -1.0, 1.0)
>>> likelihood = numpy.array([[0.2, 0.5, 0.8], [1.0, 1.0, 1.0]])
>>> posterior(numpy.log(likelihood), grid, uniform_log_prior(-1.0, 1.0)).round(4)
array([[0.1333, 0.3333, 0.5333],
       [0.3333, 0.3333, 0.3333]])
