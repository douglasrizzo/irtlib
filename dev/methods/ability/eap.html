Expected A Posteriori#

API:

irtlib.ability.expected_a_posteriori()

Sources:

Bock and Mislevy [1982]; Magis and Raîche [2012]

Motivation#

The expected a posteriori (EAP) estimate is the mean of the examinee’s posterior, the likelihood of its responses times a prior density of ability, and its standard error is the posterior standard deviation. It needs no search, only sums over a grid of abilities, and it is finite whatever the responses. Maximum A Posteriori takes the posterior’s mode instead.

Definition#

The EAP estimate is the mean of the examinee’s posterior over the grid of Quadrature and Ability Priors, and its standard error the posterior standard deviation [Bock and Mislevy, 1982, p. 433, Equations 4 and 5]; [Magis and Raîche, 2012, pp. 5-6, Equations 10 and 11].

Implementation#

The posterior is that of Quadrature and Ability Priors: the masses of the prior on the grid, times the likelihood at each node, normalized, with no correction of the variance. It is finite whatever the responses, and an examinee who answered no item gets the prior’s mean and standard deviation on the grid. A caller that estimates many matrices with the same grid and prior can pass a GridPrior built once instead of the two.

Comparison with mirt and catR

  • mirt 1.48: irtlib’s default grid and prior are mirt’s, 121 nodes on \([-6, 6]\) (R/fscores.R:302, 318-319; R/fscores.internal.R:231) with the standard normal density at the nodes, normalized (R/fscores.internal.R:316-317). mirt gives NA to an examinee who answered no item.

  • catR 3.17: the parity tests first check that catR’s recorded EAP estimates are the posterior mean and standard deviation written out, on the same nodes and weights, then compare irtlib’s with them (ADR-0003).

Guarantees#

  1. Every estimate is finite, whatever the responses, and lies within the grid’s nodes.

  2. An examinee who answered no item gets the prior’s mean and standard deviation on the grid.

API Reference#

irtlib.ability.expected_a_posteriori(responses: ArrayLike, items: NDArray[floating[Any]], *, grid: QuadratureGrid | None = None, log_prior: LogPrior | None = None, grid_prior: GridPrior | None = None) → AbilityEstimates[source]#

Return each examinee’s EAP estimate, the mean of its posterior over a grid, with the posterior standard deviation.

Every estimate is finite, whatever the responses; an examinee who answered no item gets the prior’s mean and standard deviation on the grid. The evaluation count is the number of grid nodes.

The Expected A Posteriori page gives the method, its sources and the origin of each default.

Parameters:
responsesarray_like

The response matrix, one row per examinee and one column per item: 1 or True for a correct response, 0 or False for an incorrect one, and NaN for a missing one.

itemsnumpy.ndarray

The items [a, b, c, d], a matrix of shape (n, 4), one row per column of responses, with a > 0 and 0 <= c < d <= 1.

gridQuadratureGrid or None, optional

The nodes the posterior is computed on. Default is QuadratureGrid.uniform(), 121 nodes on [-6, 6].

log_priorLogPrior or None, optional

The prior’s log-density. Default is normal_log_prior(), the standard normal.

grid_priorGridPrior or None, optional

A grid and prior evaluated once, in place of grid and log_prior, for a caller that estimates with the same ones many times. Default is None.

Returns:
AbilityEstimates

The posterior means, the posterior standard deviations and the grid’s size, one of each per examinee.

Raises:
TypeError

If the responses are neither Booleans, integers nor floats.

ValueError

If grid_prior is given together with grid or log_prior. Also if items is not an (n, 4) matrix, responses is not a matrix with one column per item, a response is not 0, 1 or NaN, or the log-prior is NaN or +inf at a node or has no mass on the grid.

Examples

>>> import numpy
>>> items = numpy.array([[1.0, -1.0, 0.0, 1.0], [1.5, 0.0, 0.2, 1.0], [0.8, 1.0, 0.0, 0.95]])
>>> estimates = expected_a_posteriori(numpy.array([[1, 0, numpy.nan], [1, 1, 1]]), items)
>>> estimates.theta.round(4), estimates.standard_errors.round(4)
(array([-0.2809,  0.8555]), array([0.7837, 0.828 ]))
