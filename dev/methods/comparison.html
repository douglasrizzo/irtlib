Model Comparison#

API:

irtlib.comparison

Sources:

Akaike [1974]; Schwarz [1978]; Vrieze [2012]; Bock and Aitkin [1981]

Motivation#

A model with more parameters always fits at least as well; the question is whether the improvement outweighs the parameters spent. The likelihood-ratio test compares two nested models, such as the 2PL and the 3PL calibrated on the same responses; the information criteria rank any models calibrated on them; and \(G^2\) tests one model against every pattern of responses. BIC selects the true model as the sample grows, when it is among the candidates, and AIC does not; AIC is efficient when no candidate is true [Vrieze, 2012, Pages 10-11].

Definition#

For \(k\) estimated parameters and the maximized log-likelihood \(\log L\):

  • the likelihood-ratio statistic of nested models is \(-2\) times the difference of their log-likelihoods, “distributed as \(\chi^2\)” with the difference in their numbers of parameters as its degrees of freedom [Orlando and Thissen, 2000, p. 51];

  • AIC is \(-2\log L + 2k\) [Akaike, 1974, p. 719];

  • BIC is \(-2\log L + k\log N\) for \(N\) examinees, Schwarz’s criterion [Schwarz, 1978, p. 461] multiplied by \(-2\), with the sample size Vrieze [2012, Page 10] uses;

  • \(G^2\) compares the model with the general multinomial over response patterns, \(2\sum_x r_x \log(r_x / (N \tilde P_x))\) over the observed patterns \(x\), observed \(r_x\) times with marginal probability \(\tilde P_x\) [Bock and Aitkin, 1981, p. 450].

Implementation#

\(G^2\)’s degrees of freedom are \(2^M - 1 - k\) for \(M\) items, as in the values Bock and Aitkin [1981, p. 455, Table 3] and Thissen [1982, p. 178] report, whatever the patterns observed; Bock and Aitkin’s Equation 16 (p. 450) counts the observed patterns instead. The multinomial is not defined when responses are missing, so \(G^2\) needs complete responses.

The log-likelihoods. With priors, the log-likelihood is the one at the Bayes modal estimates, without the log-prior. Every statistic here takes marginal log-likelihoods, and refuses a calibration of another likelihood, such as the conditional one of Conditional Maximum Likelihood, which is not on their scale (ADR-0006).

Comparison with mirt

  • mirt 1.48 counts \(G^2\)’s degrees of freedom as \(2^M - 1 - k\) too.

  • On calibrations of the same responses, the parity tests find the numbers of parameters equal to mirt’s, the log-likelihoods, AIC and BIC within a relative \(10^{-10}\), \(G^2\) and the likelihood-ratio statistic within \(10^{-6}\), and their degrees of freedom equal (ADR-0003).

Parameters#

The functions take calibrations; bic() also takes the number of examinees, and g2() the responses. None has a setting.

Guarantees#

  1. A calibration whose likelihood is not marginal raises an error.

  2. The likelihood-ratio test raises an error unless the general model has more parameters than the restricted one, on the same items.

  3. \(G^2\) of responses with a missing entry raises an error.

irtlib.comparison.likelihood_ratio(restricted: Calibration, general: Calibration) → ChiSquareTest[source]#

Return the likelihood-ratio test of a restricted model against a more general one it is nested in.

The two must be calibrations of the same responses, the restricted model a special case of the general one, such as the 1PL of the 2PL; only the numbers of items and parameters can be checked. The test of a parameter on the boundary of its range, such as the 3PL’s guessing parameter at 0 against the 2PL, is the ordinary one, whose p-value is then conservative. Calibrations with priors are compared by their log-likelihoods without the log-prior, and a restricted model that fits better, as when a calibration stopped short, gives a negative statistic and a p-value of 1.

Parameters:
restrictedCalibration

The calibration of the restricted model, of the marginal likelihood.

generalCalibration

The calibration of the general model, of the marginal likelihood.

Returns:
ChiSquareTest

Twice the gain in log-likelihood, on the difference in the numbers of parameters.

Raises:
ValueError

If the two calibrations have different numbers of items, the general model does not have more parameters, or a calibration’s likelihood is not marginal.
